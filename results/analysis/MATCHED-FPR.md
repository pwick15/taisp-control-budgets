# Comparing monitors at a false-positive rate they can actually deliver

Generated by `scripts/analysis_matched_fpr.py`. No API cost; re-run it any time.

## The problem

`compute_safety(df, scorer, budget)` sets its cutoff with
`np.percentile(honest, 100 - budget)`, which interpolates. Our monitors emit a
handful of distinct scores, and the cutoff is applied with a strict `>`, so the
only false-positive rates a threshold can realise are `mean(honest > v)` over
those distinct values. A requested budget falling between two of them lands on
no real operating point, and **different monitors round to different places**.

At the 2% headline budget, the monitors in this study actually spend between
**0.0% and 2.0%** FPR. Ranking them at that nominal budget hands a bigger audit
budget to whichever monitor happened to round up.

The clearest case is the headline pair:

| monitor | nominal budget | FPR actually spent | recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sonnet 5.5 | 2% | **1.00%** | 86.8% |
| Haiku 5.5 · xhigh | 2% | **1.60%** | 85.0% |

Haiku's 85.0% was bought with 60% more audit budget than Sonnet's 86.8%. The
published line, "the 95% intervals of the two overlap, so we cannot separate
them", compares them at different prices.

## What happens when you match the FPR

Every FPR the two can both realise exactly:

| shared FPR | honest rows above cutoff | Sonnet | Haiku xhigh | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20% | 1 | 70.8% | 10.4% | +60.4 |
| 0.40% | 2 | 80.2% | 50.8% | +29.4 |
| 0.60% | 3 | 83.4% | 57.4% | +26.0 |

Sonnet leads at every one, by 26 to 60 points rather than the 1.8 the nominal
comparison suggests.

## But the honest conclusion is stronger, and it is not "Sonnet wins by 26"

Look at the third column. At 0.20% FPR on 500 honest rows the cutoff rests on
**one observation**; at 0.60%, on three. A threshold estimated from one to three
samples is barely estimated at all, and the paired bootstrap says so: the gap at
0.60% is +26.0 points with a 95% interval of **[+1.2, +71.0]**.

So we apply two conditions before calling a pair separated: the interval must
clear zero by more than one sample's worth, **and** the cutoff must rest on at
least 5 honest rows. Under that test:

- 40 monitors are compared against Sonnet at some shared FPR
- **12 share an operating point resting on >=5 honest rows**
- **Haiku 5.5 xhigh is among them**, through its wider-scale arms
- eleven of the twelve sit at 1.00% on exactly 5 honest rows; the twelfth is the
  one comfortable rung in the study, and it belongs to the weakest monitor

> **Updated 2026-11-01, and the original finding has been overturned by the fix
> this document recommended.** As first written, only 3 monitors shared an
> adequately powered rung with Sonnet, Haiku 5.5 xhigh was not one of them, and
> the conclusion was that "the two leading monitors have no common operating
> point at which the comparison is adequately powered". That was true of the
> 0-10 prompt. It is no longer true: once Haiku's 0-100 and 0-200 arms exist,
> both share the 1.00% rung with Sonnet on 5 honest rows and both separate from
> zero. The score-resolution fix argued for below is what did it, so the right
> reading is not that the original claim was wrong but that it described a
> limitation the project has since removed. The limitation is still real at
> 0-10, and still worth stating as such.

Eleven of the twelve rest on exactly 5 honest rows, which is the bar and not a
comfortable margin, and the intervals are correspondingly wide. The two Haiku
entries are the interesting ones: at a matched 1.00% false-positive rate Sonnet
leads its 0-100 arm by 17.0 points and its 0-200 arm by 8.8, both separated from
zero but with lower bounds of +1.4 and +0.4. Treat them as "separated, barely"
rather than as a measured gap.

The Gemma 3 4B row is the exception and it is instructive. It is the only pair in
the study whose shared cutoff rests on a comfortable number of honest rows, 46,
and the interval is correspondingly the narrowest. It gets there by being so
coarse that the only rate it and Sonnet can both express is 9.20%, which is 9x
the headline budget and not an operating point anyone would deploy. So the one
well-powered comparison the study owns is between the best monitor and the worst
at a price nobody would pay, which is the clearest statement available of why
resolution and not sample size is the binding constraint.

## Why this points straight at the score-resolution fix

Monitors share few operating points because each has only a handful. More
distinct score values would mean a denser achievable grid, more shared rungs,
and shared rungs high enough that the cutoff rests on enough honest rows to
estimate. In E0 (`experiments/e0-monitor-resolution/`, Haiku 4.5, n=200) the
0-100 prompt moved the monitor from 3 distinct honest scores to 7, and its
budget floor from 2.00% to 0.50%, at +2.8% cost.

Two cautions on reading that across documents. The experiment is E0 at n=200,
not one of the n=500 runs, where the same change is smaller and
monitor-dependent: Sonnet 5.5 goes from 6 distinct honest scores to 7 and Haiku
5.5 xhigh from 7 to 14. And "budget floor" is used in two senses in this
project. Here it is the **smallest achievable non-zero false-positive rate**,
which is what `src/e0_analyze.py` computes. In `SCORE-SCALE.md` it is the
**lowest usable rung**, meaning the cheapest rate whose cutoff still rests on at
least 5 honest rows, which is a different and stricter quantity: under that
definition the same two n=500 pairs read 1.0% to 1.2% for Sonnet and 1.6% to
1.0% for Haiku. Both numbers are correct for their own definition, and neither
transfers to the other document.

So this is not two separate work items. **The matched-FPR comparison is the
reason the resolution fix matters**: without it, the study's central comparison
between its two best monitors is not merely close, it is unmeasurable.

## How to read the outputs

- `matched-fpr-ranking.csv`: for each target FPR, every monitor's best
  achievable point at or below it, with the achieved FPR and the shortfall, so
  any residual mismatch is visible rather than hidden.
- `matched-fpr-pairwise.csv`: Sonnet against each monitor at exactly-shared
  FPRs. `honest_rows_above_cutoff` is the adequacy check; `separated` already
  applies both conditions above.

The pairwise bootstrap joins the two runs on `sample_id`, so both monitors score
the same problems, and re-estimates both thresholds inside every replicate.
